26. Explorando as Condições: Zero e First Conditional

26.1 Verdades Universais e Fatos: Zero Conditional 🌍✔️

O Zero Conditional é usado para falar sobre coisas que são sempre verdadeiras, como fatos científicos, leis da natureza, hábitos ou situações que sempre produzem a mesma consequência.

If / When + Simple Present, ... Simple Present

Podemos usar "when" (quando) no lugar de "if" (se) no Zero Conditional com exatamente o mesmo sentido de certeza.

26.2 Situações Reais ou Prováveis no Futuro: First Conditional 🔮➡️

O First Conditional é usado para falar sobre situações reais ou prováveis no futuro e suas consequências diretas. A condição tem chance real de acontecer.

If + Simple Present, ... will / can / may / might / should + Verbo (base)

🚨 Duas Regras de Ouro das Condicionais (Zero e Tipo 1)

  • Regra da Vírgula: Quando a frase começa com If, usamos vírgula no meio (If it rains, I'll stay home). Quando o if vai no meio, não usamos vírgula (I'll stay home if it rains).
  • Proibido "Will" depois de "If": Mesmo falando do futuro, o verbo logo depois de If fica no Presente Simples! Diga "If it rains tomorrow, I will stay home" (NUNCA "If it will rain" ❌).

26.4 Tarefa Extra: Leitura, Áudio e Quiz Interativo ✍️🎧

Leia e escute o texto abaixo sobre planejar uma viagem de fim de semana. Observe como o Zero Conditional (regras gerais) e o First Conditional (planos para este fim de semana) trabalham juntos!

📖 Story: Planning a Weekend Trip

When my friends and I plan a weekend trip, we always check the weather first. This is a general rule for us because if the weather is bad, we usually change our plans. If it snows in the mountains, the roads get very dangerous. That's a scientific fact. Also, when you don't prepare for a trip properly, things often go wrong.

For this upcoming weekend, our plan depends completely on the forecast. If the forecast is good, we will go hiking. The trail is beautiful, but it gets muddy if it rains. So, if we see dark clouds on Saturday morning, we will choose an indoor activity instead. We will probably go to the local museum.

My friend, Ana, is in charge of the food. If she cooks, everyone is always happy because her food is delicious. For this trip, if we go hiking, she will prepare sandwiches and snacks. If we stay in the city, we will try that new restaurant downtown.

I am responsible for transportation. If my car is ready from the mechanic, I will drive. If it isn't ready, we will have to take the train. The train is a good option, but if we take it, we won't be as flexible with our schedule.

Ultimately, if we plan everything carefully, we will have a great time, no matter the weather. When friends travel together, the most important thing is the company. If we are together, it will be a memorable weekend.

Parte 1: Zero ou First Conditional?

Digite Z (Zero Conditional) ou F (First Conditional) nos campos abaixo:

  2. If she studies hard, she will get good grades. ()
  3. When I am sad, I listen to music. ()
  4. We will miss the train if we don't leave now. ()

Parte 2: Complete as Frases Condicionais

Complete as lacunas com a forma correta do verbo entre parênteses:

  1. If it (rain) tomorrow, we (stay) home.
  2. Water (boil) if you (heat) it to 100°C.
  3. If I (see) her, I (tell) her you called.
📖 Ver Gabarito dos Exercícios com Áudio 🔊

Parte 1:

1. Z (Zero Conditional — fato científico). — If you heat ice, it melts.

2. F (First Conditional — consequência provável no futuro com will). — If she studies hard, she will get good grades.

3. Z (Zero Conditional — hábito pessoal sempre verdadeiro). — When I am sad, I listen to music.

4. F (First Conditional — plano/consequência futura). — We will miss the train if we don't leave now.

Parte 2:

1. If it rains tomorrow, we will stay / 'll stay home.

2. Water boils if you heat it to 100°C.

3. If I see her, I will tell / 'll tell her you called.
